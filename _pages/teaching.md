#### Teaching philosophy

I teach mathematics as a way of reasoning about the world, not a list of procedures. Many of my students take calculus or statistics as a requirement for business, life-science, or social-science majors, so I build lessons around questions they recognize, such as how fast an outbreak is growing, how a price responds to demand, or how much a survey result should be trusted, and then develop the mathematics needed to answer them. I start from concrete examples, ask students to predict before we compute, and use frequent short checks so that misunderstandings surface early, when they are easiest to correct.

Having taught in Nigeria and the United States, from high school to university courses as a teaching assistant, co-instructor, and instructor of record, I have learned that students arrive with very different preparation, and that clear structure and steady encouragement matter as much as content. I set high expectations, explain the reasoning behind every method, and make myself available so that every student has a real path to meeting them. My research in mathematical modeling gives me a steady supply of real data and applications, and I bring these into class so students can see mathematics being used to inform decisions in public health and natural resource management.
